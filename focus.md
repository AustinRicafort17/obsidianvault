## Protocols 
ChatGibbity Says:

1. Time-blocking protocol

Work in defined intervals rather than “until you’re done.”

- 25 minutes focused work + 5-minute break (Pomodoro)
- Or 50 minutes work + 10-minute break for deeper tasks
- After 3–4 cycles, take a 20–30 minute break.

2. Single-task protocol

Before starting:

- Pick one specific outcome.
- Close unrelated tabs and apps.
- Silence notifications or enable Do Not Disturb.
- Keep only the materials you need visible.

3. Attention reset protocol

When you notice your mind wandering:

1. Stop for a few seconds.
2. Identify the distraction (“thinking about email,” “checking my phone”).
3. Redirect your attention to the next concrete action.
4. Continue without judging yourself for losing focus.

5. Environment protocol

Reduce friction for concentrating:

- Quiet workspace or consistent background noise.
- Comfortable lighting.
- Phone out of reach if it’s a frequent distraction.
- Water nearby to avoid unnecessary interruptions.

5. Task breakdown protocol

If a task feels overwhelming:

- Define the first 2-minute action.
- Break the project into chunks of 15–30 minutes.
- Celebrate completion of each chunk before moving on.

6. Energy management protocol

Attention depends on physical state.  
Aim for:

- Regular sleep.
- Balanced meals and hydration.
- Movement breaks every hour or two.
- Limiting heavy multitasking when you’re mentally fatigued.

7. Daily planning protocol

At the beginning of the day:

- Identify your top 1–3 priorities.
- Schedule your most demanding work during the time of day when you typically feel most alert.
- Batch similar tasks (emails, messages, meetings) together.

8. Digital distraction protocol

- Turn off nonessential notifications.
- Use website/app blockers during focus sessions if needed.
- Check email and messaging at scheduled times instead of continuously.

9. Review protocol

At the end of the day, spend 5 minutes asking:

- What helped me focus?
- What distracted me most?
- What will I change tomorrow?

If attention problems are persistent

If you consistently struggle to focus across work, school, and daily life—and it’s affecting your functioning—it may be worth discussing it with a healthcare professional. Attention difficulties can have many causes, including stress, anxiety, depression, sleep problems, medication effects, or conditions such as ADHD. A proper evaluation can help identify the cause and guide appropriate strategies or treatment.

The most effective protocol is usually one that’s simple enough to repeat consistently rather than the most elaborate one. Even a routine of 45 minutes of focused work, a 10-minute break, and minimizing distractions can substantially improve sustained attention over time.
