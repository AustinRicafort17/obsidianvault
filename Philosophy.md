The previous list is historically respectable, but I’d change the structure.

You shouldn’t simply go:

Plato → Aristotle → Augustine → Descartes → Hume → Kant → Nietzsche → Heidegger

and call that a complete philosophy education.

That gives you a history of one major philosophical tradition, not philosophy as a whole.

You need to add:

- Logic
- Epistemology
- Metaphysics
- Ethics
- Political philosophy
- Philosophy of mind
- Philosophy of science
- Philosophy of language
- Philosophy of mathematics
- Philosophy of religion
- Chinese philosophy
- Indian philosophy
- African/Africana philosophy
- Contemporary analytic philosophy

The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy’s current coverage itself reflects how enormous the field is: epistemology, ethics, logic, philosophy of mind, philosophy of science, Chinese philosophy, Indian philosophy, Africana philosophy, etc.

And here’s the really important part

Don’t buy all 60 philosophy books at once.

Some of those are books you study, not books you casually read. Critique of Pure Reason, Phenomenology of Spirit, Being and Time, Process and Reality, etc. are intellectual boss fights. 😂

A good philosophy education alternates:

accessible explanation → primary source → explanation/commentary → primary source

rather than just stacking difficult classics.

And for philosophy, the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy⁠ is an excellent companion. Its entries are written as scholarly reference material and its coverage spans the major historical and contemporary areas of the field. 

Bottom line

Original science list: 6/10 for education, 9/10 for interesting popular reading.

Revised science list: much closer to an actual self-education curriculum.

Original philosophy list: 7.5/10, but heavily Western-canon focused.

Revised philosophy list: much closer to a genuine broad foundation.

If you’re actually building a personal library to educate yourself from roughly high-school level all the way toward university/graduate-level thinking, I’d go one step further and make you a master reading curriculum across Science + Math + Philosophy + History + Economics + Psychology, with the books arranged in the exact order I’d read them.
